# Questions and answers

### Is this medical advice?

No. Clockblock gives general information about timing light, sleep and caffeine, based on published research.
It isn't a diagnosis or treatment. It's meant for healthy adults, not for flight crew on duty. If you have a
health condition, are pregnant, or take medication, talk to a doctor before you change your sleep.

### Does it need the internet?

No. The app doesn't have internet permission at all. Airport search, planning, reminders and widgets all work in
flight mode.

### Do I need an account?

No. There are no accounts, no ads and no tracking. See [Settings and your data](settings-and-your-data.md#your-data)
for where your data is kept.

### How does it work?

Your body clock follows light. Light at the right time of your body's day moves the clock earlier, and light at
another time moves it later. The plan works out where your body clock is, then tells you when to look for light
and when to avoid it so the clock moves the right way, at a pace the body can manage (about 1 to 1.5 hours a day
earlier, or up to two hours a day later). Sleep, naps and caffeine are fitted around that. In the app, **About
Clockblock** explains this in more detail, and every block has a **Why?**.

For the full science, see the [research review](../docs/science.md).

### Why does the plan ask me to wear sunglasses at noon?

Because at that moment, light would push your body clock the wrong way. The time on the wall doesn't matter here;
what matters is the time your body thinks it is. Tap **Why?** on the block to see the reason.

### I missed a block. Is my plan ruined?

No. Mark it **Skipped** or **Can't do this** and carry on with the next block. One missed block just makes
adapting take a little longer. The plan doesn't change when you skip something.

### My flight is delayed. What do I do?

Open the trip, tap **Edit**, and use the **I'm delayed** card at the top. It appears from 48 hours before
departure until you land. See [Planning a trip](planning-a-trip.md#when-your-flight-is-delayed).

### It's a short trip. Do I have to adapt?

Usually not. If you add your return flight and you fly back within 72 hours, the plan keeps your body on home time
instead. You can choose this yourself under **Body clock** in the trip editor.

### Why is there no melatonin in my plan?

Melatonin is off by default. To turn it on, go to **Settings** → **Tools** → **Melatonin** and read the safety note
first. Even when it's on, the plan only suggests melatonin when it helps, which is when your clock needs to move
earlier. Melatonin is a medicine in many countries: check with a doctor or pharmacist before you take it.

### Why did I get no reminder during the night?

On purpose. Reminders never arrive while the plan says you should be asleep, except the one that wakes you up.

### Reminders arrive late or not at all.

Check **Settings** → **What Android allows**. See
[If reminders don't arrive](widgets-and-notifications.md#if-reminders-dont-arrive).

### Can I move my trips to a new phone?

Yes. Use **Export a backup** on the old phone and **Import a backup** on the new one. See
[Back up and restore](settings-and-your-data.md#back-up-and-restore).

### Can I see the plan in my calendar?

Yes. In a plan, open the menu and choose **Export to calendar**.

### Is the app free?

Yes. It's free and open source under the Apache License 2.0. The code is on
[GitHub](https://github.com/rock3r/clockblock).

### Are there any surprises hidden in the app?

A few. The ones on the plan screen stay hidden while your plan says sleep or when Reduce motion is on.
